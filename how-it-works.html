Tournament guide

How the 256-team tournament works

One enormous field, eight straight knockout rounds and no second chances. This guide explains what happens from the opening draw to the final whistle.

Written and maintained by Mohamed A. · Updated 29 July 2026

The format in one minute

The main 256 TEAMS WC mode is a single-elimination football tournament. A field of exactly 256 sides creates a balanced bracket: 128 opening ties become 64, then 32, 16, 8, 4, 2 and finally one champion. Every winner advances and every loser is eliminated.

Because 256 is a power of two, there are no byes and no preliminary rounds. The champion must win eight matches. Across the complete tournament, 255 ties are played to eliminate 255 teams; the interface may describe the journey using the 256-team field as its central theme.

Quick route: choose a simulation style and goal level, decide whether to manage a country or stay neutral, then start the tournament. You can watch ties, reveal results one at a time or simulate a whole round.

Who enters the field?

The default field combines 211 FIFA member associations with 45 clearly labelled guest sides. The guests include islands, territories, micronations and representative teams that make the bracket reach 256 without pretending they are FIFA members.

This is a fictional global competition, not an official qualification system. The expanded field is the point: it lets visitors follow familiar contenders, discover smaller football nations and see how far an unlikely run can go. The participant browser on the homepage shows the complete field before the first match.

Draw, matches and progression

The opening draw

A new tournament creates an opening bracket from the available teams. Once play begins, each tie has a fixed route through the bracket, so you can follow a country’s possible opponents all the way to the final.

Resolving a tie

Matches are simulated as football contests rather than coin flips. Team quality influences the balance of chances, while controlled variation leaves room for draws, narrow wins and upsets. A tied knockout match proceeds through extra time and, if still level, a penalty shootout so every tie produces one winner.

Following the action

You can play a match live with a visible clock, goals, cards, commentary and playback controls. If you prefer a faster tournament, reveal the result immediately or simulate the remaining ties in a round. The bracket, Golden Boot table and tournament storylines update as results arrive.

Choosing how to play

  • Neutral spectator: see every match normally and follow the whole field without controlling one country.
  • Manage a country: centre the journey on one team, choose tactics where available and decide what to do after elimination.
  • Spoiler-safe viewing: keep a result concealed until you are ready to reveal it, useful for reaction videos or shared sessions.
  • Whole-round simulation: advance a large bracket quickly while retaining every fixture and result.

Progress is stored in your browser for the main local tournament. Signed-in features can also track achievements and selected online records. The mode guide explains how the custom, online, legacy and historical competitions differ.

What the result means

A simulated champion is an entertainment outcome, not a forecast. Ratings and player pools are curated for a believable experience, but every match contains randomness and the model intentionally simplifies real football. Running the same matchup again can produce a different story.
